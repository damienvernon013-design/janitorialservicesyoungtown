Cleaning Considerations for Facilities With Shared Equipment

Shared equipment and workstations carry higher pathogen loads than private offices — the cleaning approach should reflect that.

Facilities where equipment or workstations are shared among multiple people — warehouses with shared tools, hot-desk offices, training rooms, and similar spaces — face a higher pathogen load on shared surfaces than a private-office environment. The cleaning approach should reflect that difference.

Why Shared Surfaces Are Higher Risk

Any surface touched by multiple people throughout the day accumulates bacteria faster than a surface used by one person. Shared keyboards, tool handles, break room equipment, and multi-user workstations fall into this category.

What a Cleaning Plan Should Cover

  • Disinfectant wipe-down of shared surfaces on a defined schedule, not just visible-dirt response
  • Floor cleaning appropriate to the surface — rubber matting, sealed concrete, or standard flooring
  • Water station and shared-appliance sanitization
  • Restroom and locker area disinfection where applicable

Working With Your Provider

If your facility has shared equipment or hot-desk arrangements, name those areas specifically in your scope of work. A provider experienced with multi-user spaces will understand the difference between routine cleaning and the disinfection discipline shared surfaces actually need.
