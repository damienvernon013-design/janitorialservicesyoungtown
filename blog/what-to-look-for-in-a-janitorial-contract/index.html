What to Look for in a Janitorial Contract Before You Sign

A contract is a legal document that defines your service relationship. Read past the price line before signing.

A janitorial contract is a legal document that defines your service relationship. Many facility managers sign after a quick skim, focused on price, and miss the clauses that determine what happens when something goes wrong.

The Essential Sections

Scope of Work

The most important section. It should itemize every task, specify the areas covered, and define frequency for each. Vague language without specifics should trigger a request for a detailed addendum.

Service Frequency and Schedule

The contract should clearly state how often service occurs, which days, and whether timing is fixed or flexible.

Pricing and Payment Terms

Confirm whether pricing is monthly, per-visit, or otherwise structured, and when invoices are issued and due. Look for escalation clauses that allow future increases.

Contract Duration and Renewal

Most contracts run for a set term and auto-renew unless cancelled within a notice window. Mark that date.

Termination Clause

Understand what it costs to exit early, and whether a mutual termination clause exists if service quality falls below standard.

Complaint and Remedy Process

How do you report a problem, and what response time and remedy — a re-clean or credit — can you expect?

Insurance and Liability

Confirm the provider carries general liability and workers’ compensation coverage, and ask for a certificate naming your business as an additional insured.

What to Negotiate

Contracts are starting points, not take-it-or-leave-it documents. Ask about trial periods, mutual termination rights, and performance benchmarks tied to your complaint process.
