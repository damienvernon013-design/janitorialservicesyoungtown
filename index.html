What a Written Scope Means in Practice

Before the first clean, a supervisor walks your facility with a clipboard. Every area is listed, every task is assigned a frequency, and every consumable decision (paper, soap, liners) is documented. That scope is what we are held to — and what you can use to hold us to it. If a task is not on the scope, it is not assumed. If you need to add it, we price it and revise the scope in writing before it changes.

Most janitorial complaints trace to the same source: no written scope, so nobody agrees on what was promised. We have been doing this for 22 years and the scope document is the single thing that keeps a contractor relationship from turning adversarial.
